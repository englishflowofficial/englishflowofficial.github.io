Master Phrasal Verbs

The Ultimate Phrasal Verb Guide

Native speakers use these every day. Memorize them to sound fluent!

1. Communication & Relationships

Catch up
To get the latest news or updates from someone.
"It’s been months! We really need to catch up over coffee this weekend."
Bring up
To start talking about a subject.
"Please don't bring up the budget during the creative meeting."
Fall out (with)
To have an argument and stop being friendly.
"She fell out with her roommate over the electricity bill."
Make up
To forgive each other and become friends again after an argument.
"They had a huge fight yesterday, but they made up this morning."
Open up
To share your true thoughts or feelings.
"It took weeks, but Arjun finally opened up about his job stress."
Hang out
To spend time together socially.
"We usually hang out at the café near the station on Saturdays."
Get along
To have a friendly, easy relationship with someone.
"My flatmate and I get along really well."
Speak up
To say something loudly or express your opinion.
"If you disagree with the plan, please speak up now."
Point out
To indicate or mention something important.
"The teacher pointed out my spelling mistakes kindly."
Get through (to)
To make someone understand you; to reach someone by phone.
"I couldn’t get through to him — he just wouldn’t listen."

2. Work & Productivity

Figure out
To find the solution to a problem.
"It took me an hour to figure out how to use this new software."
Put off
To delay doing something; to procrastinate.
"Don't put off your homework until Sunday night."
Call off
To cancel an event or meeting.
"The manager called off the meeting because everyone was sick."
Look over
To examine or review something quickly.
"Could you look over my presentation before I send it?"
Take on
To accept extra work or responsibility.
"She took on two new projects this month."
Get ahead
To make progress and become successful at work.
"Working late every day won’t help you get ahead."
Carry out
To perform or complete a task.
"The team carried out the safety checks before the launch."
Run by
To discuss an idea with someone for their opinion or approval.
"Let me run this proposal by my manager first."
Hand in
To submit work or documents.
"You must hand in the report by Friday evening."
Keep up
To stay at the same pace or level as others.
"The training is fast — I can barely keep up."

3. Movement & Travel

Take off
When an airplane leaves the ground.
"Please fasten your seatbelt. The plane is about to take off."
Drop off
To take someone or something to a destination.
"I need to drop off this package at the post office."
Set off
To begin a journey.
"We set off early in the morning to avoid the traffic."
Check in
To arrive and register at a hotel or airport.
"We need to check in at least two hours before our flight."
Check out
To leave and pay at a hotel; to look at something.
"We checked out at noon and left our bags at reception."
Get in
To arrive at a destination.
"Our train gets in at 9:30 tonight."
Get off
To leave a bus, train, or plane.
"Get off at the third stop — the office is right there."
Get on
To board a bus, train, or plane.
"We got on the metro just before the doors closed."
Look around
To explore a new place.
"We had an hour, so we looked around the old market."
Speed up
To go or move faster.
"The driver sped up to cross the signal before it turned red."

4. Daily Life & Home

Get up
To leave your bed in the morning.
"I get up at 6 AM and go for a walk."
Turn on
To start a device or machine.
"Can you turn on the fan? It’s so hot."
Turn off
To stop a device or machine.
"Don’t forget to turn off the lights before you leave."
Clean up
To make a place tidy.
"We cleaned up the kitchen after dinner."
Throw away
To put something in the bin.
"These old newspapers are useless — throw them away."
Put away
To return something to its proper place.
"He put away the groceries as soon as he came home."
Pick up
To collect or tidy things.
"Pick up your toys before your grandmother arrives."
Eat out
To eat at a restaurant instead of at home.
"We’re too tired to cook — let’s eat out tonight."
Drop by
To visit someone briefly without planning.
"I was in your area, so I dropped by to say hello."
Move out
To leave the place where you live.
"She moved out of the hostel after her first year."

5. Money & Shopping

Pay off
To finish paying money you owe.
"He finally paid off his bike loan last month."
Pay back
To return money you borrowed.
"I’ll pay you back by the end of this week."
Save up
To collect money over time for something.
"She’s saving up for a new laptop."
Run out (of)
To use up all of something so nothing remains.
"We’ve run out of milk — can you buy some?"
Cut back (on)
To reduce spending or use of something.
"We’re cutting back on eating out to save money."
Chip in
To contribute money or effort for something shared.
"Everyone chipped in for the farewell gift."
Rip off
To charge far too much money unfairly.
"That shop ripped me off — ₹500 for a bottle of water!"
Sell out
To have sold every item, with none left.
"The concert tickets sold out in ten minutes."
Go up
To increase in price.
"Vegetable prices have gone up again this month."
Come down
To decrease in price.
"Hopefully phone prices come down after the festival sale."

6. Health & Feelings

Work out
To exercise to stay fit.
"He works out at the gym every morning."
Calm down
To become relaxed after being angry or upset.
"Take a deep breath and calm down — we can fix this."
Cheer up
To become happier; to make someone happier.
"She cheered up when she heard the good news."
Get over
To recover from an illness or emotional shock.
"It took me two weeks to get over the flu."
Burn out
To become exhausted from too much work.
"He burned out after three months of 14-hour days."
Slow down
To do things more calmly and gently.
"You need to slow down and take care of your health."
Pass out
To faint or lose consciousness.
"It was so hot in the queue that she almost passed out."
Come down (with)
To become ill with something.
"I think I’m coming down with a cold."
Freak out
To panic or react very strongly.
"My mother freaked out when she saw my exam marks."
Wind down
To relax after a busy day.
"A short walk helps me wind down before bed."

7. Learning & Thinking

Think over
To consider something carefully before deciding.
"Take a few days to think over the job offer."
Look into
To investigate or examine something.
"I’ll look into cheaper flights for next month."
Catch on
To begin to understand something.
"She caught on to the new software very quickly."
Brush up (on)
To improve or refresh your knowledge of something.
"I need to brush up on my grammar before the interview."
Come up with
To think of an idea or plan.
"We need to come up with a better marketing slogan."
Rule out
To decide something is not possible.
"The doctor ruled out anything serious."
Zone out
To stop paying attention and stare blankly.
"Sorry, I zoned out during the lecture."
Go over
To review or study something again.
"Let’s go over the notes once more before the test."
Take in
To understand or absorb information.
"There was too much to take in during the first class."
Think through
To consider all the details of a plan.
"We must think through every step before we launch."

8. Time & Plans

Show up
To arrive at a place, often unexpectedly.
"Only five people showed up for the meeting."
Run late
To be behind schedule.
"The bus is running late again today."
Push back
To delay an event to a later time.
"They pushed the meeting back to Thursday."
Back out
To decide not to do something you promised.
"He backed out of the trip at the last minute."
Hold off
To wait before doing something.
"Let’s hold off on buying until the price drops."
Wind up
To end up in a situation, often unexpectedly.
"We missed the bus and wound up walking home."
Die down
To become quieter or weaker.
"Let’s wait until the rain dies down."
Turn up
To appear or arrive, often surprisingly.
"He turned up at the party two hours late."
Wait up
To stay awake waiting for someone.
"Don’t wait up for me — I’ll be back very late."
Stay up
To not go to bed; remain awake.
"The kids stayed up until midnight on Diwali."

9. Problems & Solutions

Sort out
To organize or solve a problem.
"We need to sort out the travel plans today."
Deal with
To handle a problem or situation.
"She deals with customer complaints every day."
Run into
To meet someone unexpectedly; to face a problem.
"We ran into a small issue with the payment."
Get around (to)
To finally do something after delaying it.
"I never got around to fixing the tap."
Bring about
To cause something to happen.
"The new policy brought about many changes."
Face up (to)
To accept and deal with something difficult.
"It’s time to face up to the truth about our spending."
Iron out
To solve small problems or differences.
"We just need to iron out a few details."
Fall through
To fail to happen; to not succeed.
"Our weekend plan fell through because of the rain."
Get past
To move beyond a difficulty.
"We’ll get past this difficult phase together."
Bounce back
To recover quickly from a setback.
"The company bounced back after the losses."

10. Technology & Social Life

Log in
To access a website or app with your account.
"I logged in to check my bank balance."
Log out
To exit your account on a website or app.
"Always log out on a shared computer."
Scroll through
To move down content on a screen.
"I scrolled through her photos for an hour."
Back up
To save a copy of your data.
"Remember to back up your files before the update."
Shut down
To turn off a computer completely.
"The laptop froze, so I had to shut it down."
Pop up
To appear suddenly on screen.
"An ad popped up in the middle of the video."
Plug in
To connect a device to power.
"My phone was dying, so I plugged it in."
Sign up
To register for something online.
"I signed up for the free English course."
Tune in
To watch or listen to a broadcast.
"Tune in tomorrow for the live session at 7 PM."
Hook up
To connect devices or people together.
"Can you help me hook up the printer to my laptop?"
