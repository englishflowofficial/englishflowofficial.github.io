    📖 5 IELTS Part 3 Tips

    • Take a clear position: Open directly with your view ("In my opinion…") before unpacking the nuance.
    • Give a reason and an example: Support every abstract claim with one concrete real-world example.
    • Use linking phrases to connect ideas: Signposts like however, on the other hand, and as a result make your argument easy to follow.
    • Compare past, present, and future: Part 3 loves comparisons — contrast how things worked twenty years ago with how they might evolve next.
    • Keep talking until the timer ends: Aim for the 55–90 word sweet spot so you develop a full, balanced answer without freezing.
    🦉 Flo's tip: If a Part 3 question feels broad, split it in two — say "On the one hand…" for one group of people, then "However…" for another!

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How does IELTS Speaking Part 3 practice work?

    Pick one of five 4-question abstract topic packs (or Shuffle All). A browser voice asks each Part 3 question aloud, a 40-second countdown ring runs while you speak (150 seconds while you type), and you get instant word-development, linking-phrase, filler, and playback feedback.

    Is the examiner a real person?

    No. Questions are read aloud using your device's browser voice (speechSynthesis in en-US), not a real human examiner or studio recording. All feedback is a practice estimate and development check only — never an official IELTS band score.

    Do I need a microphone?

    No. A microphone enables live speech recognition, a volume bar, and per-answer audio playback, but if mic access is denied or unsupported, sticky typed mode lets you finish the full 4-question round with a longer 150-second timer per answer and instant text feedback.

    How do XP and daily streaks work?

    Completing a 4-question round earns +20 XP, plus a +10 XP bonus when all 4 answers reach 55 or more words. Your XP and daily streak are saved locally in your browser.
