Be, Been & Being Explained Simply

Lesson Notes: How to Use Be, Been & Being

In this session from the English Flow Podcast, we break down the difference between be, been, and being in real, everyday conversations:

  • Be: Base form. Used with modals and future actions. "Please be on time." / "I will be there soon."
  • Been: Past participle. Used after have/has/had for completed experiences or ongoing states. "I have been busy all morning."
  • Being: Continuous/present participle. Used for temporary behavior or passive voice. "Why are you being so quiet?" / "The room is being cleaned."

💡 Pro Tip: Mimic the pronunciation out loud right after each sentence. Shadowing trains your tongue and vocal cords to speak without translating in your head.
