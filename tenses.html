START WITH THE FEELING

Every tense answers
one small question.

When did it happen — and is it finished, still going, or connected to now? That is the whole idea.

Past

It already happened. Finished, or happening around a moment before now.

→ See past tenses

Present

Your routines, this exact moment, and the past that still touches today.

→ See present tenses

Future

Plans, promises and predictions — what will be true later.

→ See future tenses
◌Simple

A fact or a complete action.
I work.

⌁Continuous

In progress, unfinished.
I am working.

✦Perfect

Looking back from a point in time.
I have worked.

◇Perfect continuous

Present tenses

PRESENT · SIMPLE

Present simple

subject + verb (+ s / es)

Habits, routines, schedules and things that are generally true.

“I check my messages every morning.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I do not check my messages at night.
  • ? Do you check your messages every morning?

always · usually · often · every day · never · on Mondays

PRESENT · CONTINUOUS

Present continuous

subject + am / is / are + verb-ing

Happening at this moment, or a temporary situation around now.

“I am learning English right now.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I am not learning French this year.
  • ? Are you learning English right now?

now · right now · at the moment · today · this week

PRESENT · PERFECT

Present perfect

subject + have / has + past participle

A past action with a result now, or an experience with no exact time.

“I have lived here for three years.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − She has not finished the course yet.
  • ? Have you ever visited London?

for · since · already · yet · just · ever · never

PRESENT · PERFECT CONTINUOUS

Present perfect continuous

subject + have / has been + verb-ing

How long something has been going on — and it is usually still going.

“I have been studying for two hours.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − He has not been sleeping well lately.
  • ? How long have you been waiting?

for · since · all day · lately · how long

Past tenses

PAST · SIMPLE

Past simple

subject + verb-ed / irregular past

A finished action at a finished time in the past.

“I called my sister yesterday.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I did not call her last night.
  • ? Did you call your sister yesterday?

yesterday · last week · in 2019 · ago · when I was young

PAST · CONTINUOUS

Past continuous

subject + was / were + verb-ing

An action in progress in the past, often interrupted by something shorter.

“I was cooking when you called.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − We were not listening to the radio.
  • ? What were you doing at 8pm?

while · when · at 8pm · all evening · as

PAST · PERFECT

Past perfect

subject + had + past participle

The earlier of two past actions — the past before the past.

“The film had already started when we arrived.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I had not seen that email before the meeting.
  • ? Had the film started when you arrived?

before · after · by the time · already · until then

PAST · PERFECT CONTINUOUS

Past perfect continuous

subject + had been + verb-ing

How long something had been happening before another past moment.

“She had been working there for a year before she moved.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − They had not been waiting long when the bus came.
  • ? How long had she been working there?

for · since · before · how long · until

Future tenses

FUTURE · SIMPLE

Future simple

subject + will + verb

Promises, offers, predictions and decisions made while you speak.

“I will call you tomorrow.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I will not forget, I promise.
  • ? Will you call me tomorrow?

tomorrow · next week · soon · I think · probably

FUTURE · CONTINUOUS

Future continuous

subject + will be + verb-ing

Something that will be in progress at a certain future moment.

“This time tomorrow I will be flying to Rome.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − I will not be working on Sunday.
  • ? Will you be using the car tonight?

at 9pm tomorrow · this time next week · all morning

FUTURE · PERFECT

Future perfect

subject + will have + past participle

Something that will be complete before a deadline in the future.

“By Friday I will have finished the report.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − We will not have arrived by six.
  • ? Will you have finished by Friday?

by then · by Friday · before · in two years

FUTURE · PERFECT CONTINUOUS

Future perfect continuous

subject + will have been + verb-ing

How long something will have been going on by a future point.

“In June I will have been teaching for ten years.”

Negative, question & signal words
  • − He will not have been living here long by then.
  • ? How long will you have been waiting?

by then · for ten years · by the time

THE ONES THAT TRIP US UP

Two tenses, one real difference.

Most tense mistakes come from three pairs. Read these once and a lot of English gets easier.

Past simple vs present perfect

Finished time

“I saw that film last night.” — the time is over, so use the past simple.

Still connected

“I have seen that film.” — when it happened does not matter, the experience counts now.

Quick test. If you can say yesterday, last week or in 2020, use the past simple.

Present simple vs present continuous

Always true

“I work in a hospital.” — your usual routine or job.

Right now

“I am working from home this week.” — temporary, happening around now.

Quick test. Ask yourself: is this my normal life, or just this moment?

Will vs going to

Decided now

“The phone is ringing — I will get it.” — a decision made as you speak.

Already planned

“I am going to start a course in May.” — a plan or clear evidence.

Quick test. Look at those clouds — it is going to rain. The evidence is right there.
